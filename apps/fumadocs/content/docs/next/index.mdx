Freenary is an open-source personal finance app you run on your own server. It links your banks, sorts each transaction into a category, and compares your spending with a monthly plan.

## What it does today

- **Bank connections.** Link your banks through a bank provider, Powens or Enable Banking. Freenary fetches new transactions when you sign in or load a page, and when you press **Sync transactions**.
- **Budget.** **Transactions** shows what came in and went out over a period, by category. **Recurring** lists subscriptions, rent and other payments that repeat.
- **Categories.** Freenary sorts transactions into built-in categories, and you can correct each one. Your own categories are for budget lines.
- **A monthly plan.** Set an amount per category in **Settings**, then compare it with what you spent.
- **The assistant.** Ask questions about your money in plain words from **Home**.

The sidebar also shows Portfolio, Analysis and Goals with a **Planned** badge. Those screens don't exist yet.

## Who runs it, who uses it

One person installs Freenary on a server: that copy is your **instance**. The account that enters the setup token from the server logs becomes the **operator**, who sets up the bank provider on the setup screen. Everyone else creates an account on the same address and sees only their own data. The operator can't see other users' finances.

## Where your data lives

Accounts, transactions, categories and plans stay in the instance's own PostgreSQL database. Data leaves it through the bank provider, the assistant's model endpoint, the classifier endpoints, email and sign-in providers, and the leaked-password check. [Security](/docs/reference/security#data) lists what each one receives.
