Set up your account the first time you sign in, protect it, and choose how Freenary looks. Everything here happens in the app, from the sign-in screen or Settings.

## First steps

The first time you sign in, Freenary asks two questions:

1. **Your tax residency.** Pick every country you pay tax in. It sets the banks you can connect. Countries other than France carry a `Limited support` badge.
2. **Your bank.** Connect one now, or press `Skip for now` and do it later from Settings. If your instance can't link banks yet, Freenary skips this question.

<Callout type="warn">

No screen changes your tax residency later, so pick every country before you go on.

</Callout>

A **First steps** checklist then sits at the bottom of the sidebar. Each item opens the right Settings pane:

| Item | Done when |
| --- | --- |
| `Connect your bank` | You have a [bank connection](/docs/use/bank-connections) |
| `Set up your budget` | Your [plan](/docs/use/budget#plan-a-budget) has a line |
| `Protect your account` | You turn on two-factor authentication or add a passkey |

The checklist goes away once all three are done, and comes back if one stops being true, for example when you remove your only passkey.

## Sign in

Type your email address on the sign-in screen. Freenary then asks for your password, or for a name and a password if you're new. Password and code rules are in [Sign-in](/docs/run/sign-in).

Your operator decides what else the screen offers:

- **A confirmation code** by email for a new account, on an instance that sends email. No code? Press `Send a new code` and check your spam folder.
- **`Sign in with a passkey`**: your device's fingerprint, face or PIN instead of a password.
- **`Continue with Google`**, **`Continue with Apple`**, **`Continue with single sign-on`**, or `Continue with` followed by another provider's name.

### Change your password

Freenary has no screen to change your password. Use `Forgot password?` on the sign-in screen: it emails you a code, and you choose a new password with it.

That link only appears on an instance that sends email. Without it, ask your operator.

Freenary also has no screen to change your name or email address, or to delete your account.

## Protect your account

Do at least one of these in Settings, `Security`.

### Turn on two-factor authentication

With two-factor on, signing in with your password also asks for a code from an authenticator app.

1. In `Two-factor authentication`, turn the switch on and confirm your password.
2. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app, and type the 6-digit code it shows.
3. Copy the recovery codes and keep them somewhere safe. Freenary shows them only once.

Each recovery code signs you in once without your phone: at sign-in, choose `Use a recovery code`. To get a new set, press `Generate new recovery codes`; the old set stops working.

At sign-in, `Remember this device for 30 days` skips the code on that browser.

Two-factor protects password sign-in only. An account created with Google, Apple or single sign-on has no password, so add a passkey instead.

### Add a passkey

In `Passkeys`, press `Add a passkey`, name it, and follow your browser's prompt. Passkeys need a recent browser on a secure (HTTPS) connection.

### The recent sign-in rule

Adding or removing a passkey, and linking or unlinking Google, Apple or single sign-on, need a sign-in from the last 15 minutes. Otherwise Freenary says `Sign in again before changing how your account is accessed: this needs a session less than 15 minutes old.` Sign out, sign back in, and try again.

## Sessions

`Active sessions`, in Settings, `Security`, lists every browser signed in to your account. Press `Revoke` to sign one out, or `Sign out other sessions` to sign out every browser but this one. How long a session lasts is in [Sign-in](/docs/run/sign-in#sessions).

## Language and theme

Freenary speaks English and French, and has a light theme, a dark theme, and one that follows your system.

Change them in Settings, `Appearance`, from the menu under your name at the bottom of the sidebar, or on the sign-in screen. Changing the language reloads the page, so save an unsaved plan first.

Both choices belong to the browser, not to your account: another browser keeps its own.
